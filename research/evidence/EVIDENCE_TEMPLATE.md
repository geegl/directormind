## B. Scene dramatic structure

Record start state, character objectives, obstacle, event point, reaction point, turn, end state, POV, and audience information. Label every field OBSERVED, INFERRED, or UNKNOWN and cite timecodes.

## C. Complete shot table

Required columns:

`shot_id | start | end | duration | shot_size | camera_angle | POV | composition | blocking | action | performance/reaction beat | event/reaction/insert/empty | camera_motion | focus/depth | light/color | sound/dialogue cue | edit_in | edit_out | cut_motivation | narrative_function | axis/eyeline/action continuity | AI_complexity | status | evidence_timecode`

Cover every cut in the selected continuous scene. Do not replace missing intervals with representative samples.

## D. Coverage and spatial design

Describe geometry, axis, camera positions, entrances/exits, shared-frame versus isolated-frame decisions, reaction priority, and spatial changes.

## E. Editing and rhythm

Report shot count, average and median duration, useful distributions, repeated patterns, and single occurrences. Statistics describe this clip only unless cross-source evidence exists.

## F. Sound and performance grammar

Record ambience, silence, overlap, sound bridges, score entry/exit, off-screen cues, reaction timing, gesture, posture, gaze, and performance escalation.

## G. Transfer candidates

For each candidate rule provide:

`rule_id | trigger | directing decision | default coverage | blocking | pacing/edit | sound | applicability | failure mode | counterexample | UNKNOWN | AI risk | fallback | evidence shots | confidence`

## H. Boundary

Separate:

- VERIFIED FACT / OBSERVED;
- EVIDENCE-BASED INFERENCE;
- HYPOTHESIS / candidate rule;
- UNKNOWN.

State what this scene cannot prove and which elements must never be copied into original work.
